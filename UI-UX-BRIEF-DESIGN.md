# 20° Restobar — UI/UX Design Brief

**Status:** Brief for concept and production design  
**Version:** 1.0 — 20 August 2026  
**Primary design question:** How can a restaurant website feel transportive without slowing down a visitor who is simply trying to choose and reserve?

## 1. Background

20° Restobar combines Bar, Restaurante, Bistro, Patio and Cocina inside one Spanish/Mediterranean hospitality concept in Düsseldorf’s Andreas Quartier. The current digital identity already has useful distinctiveness: a day/night duality, blue/sand tones, cinematic imagery and room-by-room storytelling. The redesign should mature these ingredients into a more editorial, accessible and conversion-aware experience.

## 2. Design challenge

Create tension between:

- Mediterranean warmth and Düsseldorf precision.
- Immersive storytelling and instant task completion.
- One destination and five different ways to experience it.
- Cinematic motion and excellent mobile performance.
- Premium character and honest, unforced hospitality.

Do not resolve these tensions with generic luxury conventions. The site should not look like a template with a serif headline, beige background, pill buttons and looping food video.

## 3. Experience promise

> **A change of temperature in the heart of Düsseldorf.**

This is a proposed creative territory, not final public copy. It connects the 20° name, atmosphere and location without inventing a factual claim.

Desired visitor reaction:

1. “This feels specific.”
2. “I understand what kind of place this is.”
3. “I can see the occasion I would come for.”
4. “Booking is obvious.”

## 4. Audience priorities

| Priority | Audience | UX emphasis |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Mobile locals planning tonight | Live facts and one-tap reserve |
| 2 | Occasion diners | Atmosphere, menus, credibility |
| 3 | Business and event hosts | Privacy, formats, logistics |
| 4 | Düsseldorf visitors | English parity, location, clear concept |
| 5 | Enthusiasts and editorial visitors | Culinary/cocktail craft and stories |

Accessibility is not a separate persona. It is a required dimension of every audience journey.

## 5. Core tasks

- Reserve a table.
- Decide whether the menu and price context fit.
- Choose an atmosphere/space.
- Check today’s hours and get directions.
- Plan a private or business event.
- Understand the culinary point of view.

## 6. Experience principles

### Clarity before theatre

Within the first viewport, show proposition, location, current/next opening and reserve/menu actions. No splash gate.

### One expressive idea per moment

A 20-degree crop, a depth reveal or a type transition may lead a section. Do not stack all three.

### Real hospitality, closely observed

Use people and process as evidence. A hand finishing a plate is stronger than a perfect isolated dish. A glance across a table is stronger than an empty staged dining room.

### Choice without taxonomy burden

Introduce Bar, Restaurante, Bistro, Patio and Cocina as occasions—not internal departments. Pair each with “best for” and service time.

### Motion follows attention

Animate when it clarifies entry, depth or relationship. Never make users wait for text or chase moving controls.

### Operational truth is brand trust

Hours, menus, availability language, address and event expectations must be current, consistent and easy to scan.

## 7. Concept direction: “Mediterranean afterimage”

The visual journey begins in mineral daylight: paper, stone, sea-grey and airy typography. As the visitor moves deeper, warm terracotta, candle amber and ink enter. Sections feel like remembered fragments of an evening rather than a literal time-of-day switch.

Key devices:

- 20-degree image cut/reveal.
- Oversized editorial typography with calm body copy.
- Cropped moments and negative space instead of card grids.
- Thin sand-coloured rules referencing menu layout and architecture.
- Selective deep-focus parallax using foreground object, human action and room context.

## 8. Homepage wireframe brief

### 01 — Hero / decision

- Art-directed real photo or poster-first short film.
- H1: clear venue/category/location statement; creative headline can support it.
- Primary: Tisch reservieren.
- Secondary: Menüs ansehen.
- Live status: “Heute …” from central hours data.
- A discreet scroll cue only if content below is not visually obvious.

### 02 — Brand proof

- Maximum 60–90 words.
- One concrete culinary or hospitality proof point.
- Asymmetrical image/text composition; no generic icon row.

### 03 — Five experiences

- Numbered editorial index, 01–05.
- Each item includes name, best-for phrase and service cue.
- Desktop focus/hover changes an adjacent media stage.
- Mobile uses stacked media with explicit links.

### 04 — Current menu

- Show representative dishes/drinks and effective date.
- Direct HTML menu actions; PDF secondary.
- Avoid fake “featured cards” if no editorial curation process exists.

### 05 — Events

- Show a real group/room setup with factual capacity cue after approval.
- CTA: Event planen / Event anfragen.

### 06 — Cocina

- Craft, people and philosophy; verify all chef/award claims.
- No unsubstantiated superlatives.

### 07 — Visit

- Current status, weekly hours, address, directions and phone.
- Compact and highly scannable.

## 9. Key screen inventory

### Required high-fidelity designs

- Homepage: compact, medium, wide, reduced-motion state.
- Global navigation: desktop, mobile open/closed, focus state.
- Experience detail: Bar and Patio as two content extremes; component rules extrapolate to others.
- Menus index and long HTML food menu.
- Events landing and form: default, error, submission, success.
- Visit page including exception notice.
- 404 and general error state.
- Cookie/consent interface.

### Required component states

- Buttons/links, header, language switch, accordion, gallery, experience index, menu section, filter, live hours, form inputs, toast/inline notice, footer.
- Default, hover, focus-visible, active, disabled, loading, error and success.

## 10. Content and microcopy guidance

- German primary; English complete and culturally natural.
- Use direct nouns and verbs. “Tisch reservieren” beats “Jetzt erleben”.
- A CTA must predict destination: “Cocktailkarte ansehen”, not “Mehr erfahren”.
- Avoid overusing “einzigartig”, “exklusiv”, “unvergesslich” and “Lebensgefühl”. Demonstrate these qualities visually and factually.
- Use Spanish words only when they belong to the brand/experience and remain understandable in context.
- Opening status: human and precise—“Heute 17:30–01:00” or “Heute geschlossen · Dienstag ab 17:30”.

## 11. Prototype requirements

Create two prototypes:

1. **Task prototype:** low/mid fidelity; reservation, menu, hours, event enquiry, locale switch. Used for usability validation.
2. **Experience prototype:** high fidelity; hero, experience index and one parallax scene with reduced-motion counterpart. Used for motion/performance validation.

Prototype testing tasks:

- Find whether 20° is suitable for drinks tonight and reserve for two.
- Find a current food menu and one dietary indicator.
- Determine which space suits a 40-person event and enquire.
- Find the venue from Düsseldorf city centre.
- Switch language while staying on Patio.

## 12. Research and validation

### Before visual lock

- 5–8 short interviews across spontaneous diners, occasion planners and event organisers.
- Analytics baseline: landing pages, device mix, outbound reservation clicks, menu downloads.
- Staff workshop: recurring phone questions, event qualification, hour/menu update process.
- Content inventory and claim verification.

### Concept validation

- Five-second comprehension test.
- Preference test evaluates brand fit, not “which is prettier”.
- Task test with at least one keyboard/screen-reader participant or specialist audit.
- Test on real mid-tier phones over throttled connection.

## 13. Definition of design-ready

Design can enter implementation when:

- Content hierarchy and flows are validated before visual polish.
- All responsive layouts and long-copy cases exist.
- Focus order and reduced-motion behavior are annotated.
- Every media placeholder has ratio, focal intent and asset status.
- Tokens and reusable components are named consistently.
- No key screen depends on undocumented magic values.
- Engineering has reviewed motion and performance feasibility.

## 14. Deliverables

- Site map and user flows.
- Content hierarchy and wireframes.
- Visual direction and moodboard using licensed/owned references.
- Token library and component states.
- Responsive high-fidelity screens.
- Motion storyboard with timing and reduced-motion equivalents.
- Clickable task and experience prototypes.
- Accessibility annotations.
- Asset/shot list and crop matrix.
- Designer QA checklist for implementation.

## 15. Anti-slop checklist

Reject a direction if it contains three or more of the following without a clear rationale:

- Oversized vague slogan without category/location context.
- Generic beige/black luxury palette.
- Repeated rounded cards for editorial content.
- Pill-shaped controls everywhere.
- Stock/AI-perfect diners or impossible food styling.
- Grain, glow, glass or gradients used as atmosphere substitutes.
- Arbitrary marquee text.
- Scroll-jacking, custom cursor or hidden navigation.
- Identical section rhythm throughout.
- Motion that delays interaction.

## 16. Inputs needed from 20°

- Brand source files and font licences.
- A real image/video library with rights and credits.
- If imagery is insufficient, approval for the production plan in `REALISTIC-VISUAL.md`.
- Authoritative hours, menus and venue/service definitions.
- Event capacity and operational limitations.
- Approved chef/team biography and claims.
- English translation owner.
