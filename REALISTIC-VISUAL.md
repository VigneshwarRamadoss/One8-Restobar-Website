# 20° Restobar — Realistic Visual & Content Production Plan

**Status:** Production brief  
**Version:** 1.0 — 20 August 2026  
**Purpose:** Ensure the finished site is built from believable, ownable visual evidence—not stock aesthetics or AI-generated hospitality clichés.

## 1. Visual truth standard

The website must show the real 20°: its rooms, team, dishes, drinks, guests, neighbourhood and light. Image quality is not measured only by sharpness or polish. It is measured by whether a visitor can imagine a credible evening there and make a confident business decision.

### Non-negotiables

- Real venue and real menu items.
- Real team members or clearly contracted talent in the real space.
- Food/drinks prepared to service standard, not invented for impossible styling.
- Natural skin, material and food texture; restrained retouching.
- Rights, consent, credit and expiry recorded per asset.
- Composition planned for responsive crops and text-safe areas.
- AI-generated images are not used to represent the venue, staff, guests, food or views.

AI may assist private previsualisation only; it is not a publishable truth source.

## 2. Production concept

### “From first light to last glass”

Shoot one coherent service arc:

1. The calm room before guests.
2. Mise en place and preparation.
3. Doors open and first aperitivo.
4. Shared food and conversation.
5. Bar energy and close details.
6. The final glass / warm room afterimage.

This provides natural light progression without forcing a literal day/night mode.

## 3. Frame-by-frame homepage visual register

Every frame below is a production unit with a purpose, asset and fallback. “Frame” means a meaningful viewport scene, not an animation frame.

| Frame | User question | Composition | Required visual | Interaction | Conversion role |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| 00 | Is the site ready? | Poster/colour field behind immediate content | Optimised hero poster | No splash; content paints instantly | Prevent abandonment |
| 01 | What is this? | Full-bleed venue/guest moment + anchored copy | Signature wide hero + mobile portrait crop | Subtle 20° reveal; reduced-motion static | Establish desire and category |
| 02 | Can I visit/book? | Compact factual rail | No decorative image required | Live hours + Reserve + Menus | Immediate action |
| 03 | Why 20°? | Narrow editorial text beside tactile detail | Hands/ceramic/ingredient macro | Gentle reveal | Brand differentiation |
| 04 | What experiences exist? | 01–05 index + media stage | Five space-defining images | Focus/hover/tap preview | Helps visitors self-select |
| 05 | What is the Bar like? | Dark reflective close/medium frame | Bartender + drink + human context | Preview only, explicit detail link | Occasion proof |
| 06 | What is Restaurante like? | Table-depth composition | Shared dish/service gesture | Preview | Dinner/date/business proof |
| 07 | What is Bistro like? | Brighter candid composition | Informal table/aperitivo | Preview | Casual-use proof |
| 08 | What is Patio like? | Layered foliage/architecture/human frame | Real courtyard in use | Preview with restrained depth | Place differentiation |
| 09 | What is Cocina? | Kitchen action, not chef pose | Team plating/finishing | Preview | Craft credibility |
| 10 | What can I order now? | Editorial menu spread | Current hero dish + cocktail + wine detail | Menu links | Reassurance before booking |
| 11 | Can you host my event? | Group/room configuration | Real private/group setup | CTA to events | Qualified lead generation |
| 12 | Who makes the experience? | Portrait/action diptych | Team member + process | Optional short story link | Human trust |
| 13 | Where and when? | High-contrast practical band | Exterior/entrance or static map crop | Directions/phone/reserve | Remove logistical doubt |
| 14 | What next? | Calm footer on ink | Logo only or restrained material texture | Full contact/legal | Completion and recovery |

### Frame acceptance rule

Each frame needs:

- one primary message;
- one primary visual idea;
- no more than one dominant interaction;
- compact, medium and wide crop behavior;
- loading and missing-asset behavior;
- alt/caption decision;
- reduced-motion behavior;
- measurable downstream action or brand role.

## 4. Experience-page visual sequences

### Bar

1. Establishing reflection across the bar.
2. Bartender hand and ingredient.
3. Guest exchange, drink present but not product-isolated.
4. Cocktail process triptych: ingredient, motion, serve.
5. Quiet end frame with related cocktail menu.

### Restaurante

1. Table/room with clear social scale.
2. Service gesture and plated dish.
3. Date/business/shared-table scenarios without staged clichés.
4. Ingredient or kitchen connection.
5. Menu + reserve conclusion.

### Bistro

1. Brighter, less formal establishing frame.
2. Aperitivo/light dish and conversation.
3. Quick lunch/after-work cue only if currently offered and verified.
4. Transition to evening energy.
5. Relevant menu and adjacent experience.

### Patio

1. Architecture/foliage establishes seclusion in the city.
2. Foreground leaf/stone, middle guest, background façade for depth.
3. Day-to-evening pair from matching angle if production allows.
4. Seasonal/weather truth—do not imply year-round use without operational confirmation.
5. Directions/reserve/availability cue.

### Cocina

1. Team in action.
2. Ingredient and preparation.
3. Leadership portrait in working context.
4. Final plate or pass.
5. Philosophy/team/careers only with current approved content.

### Priority A — launch-critical

| Venue establishing | 2 per space | Wide | People present; clean but lived-in |
| Human interaction | 8–12 | Mixed | Consent; natural direction |
| Menu hero dishes | 6–8 | Square + portrait | Current launch menu only |
| Cocktails/wine | 5–7 | Mixed | Condensation/reflection, true recipe |
| Kitchen/process | 8–10 | Mixed | Hands, flame, pass, team |

| Exterior/arrival | 3–4 | Wide + portrait | Recognisable entrance and context |

### Priority B — depth and future campaigns

- Ingredient still life.
- Material/architectural macro library.
- Seasonal Patio pair.
- Guest arrival and coat/host gesture.
- Detail motion clips: pour, garnish, steam, door/light.
- Social crops and Open Graph compositions.

## 6. Motion capture

If video is produced, capture 4K source but deliver short, modular loops.

- 24/25 fps for cinematic base; high frame rate only for a specific slow-motion shot.
- 3–6 second usable actions; avoid long generic walkthroughs.
- Locked or controlled movement; no excessive gimbal drift.
- Record ambient sound for archive, but the website defaults muted and does not require audio.
- Hold the first frame for poster matching and clean looping.
- Capture vertical-safe alternatives; do not centre-crop all desktop footage.

## 7. Image treatment

- Natural colour with warm highlights and credible neutral shadows.
- Preserve texture; avoid crushed blacks, orange skin, excessive clarity and HDR halos.
- Grain only if it comes from a consistent photographic grade, not a CSS overlay.
- Remove temporary clutter but do not fabricate architecture, food or attendance.
- Keep label/brand accuracy on bottles where publication rights allow.
- Final colour review on calibrated display and representative phones.

## 8. Responsive crop specification

For each launch-critical asset, the photographer/designer records:

```text
Asset ID
Scene / subject
Rights owner + consent
Wide focal point (x/y)
Medium focal point (x/y)
Compact focal point (x/y)
Text-safe region
Alt decision DE
Alt decision EN
Credit
Expiry / restrictions
```

Cropping must preserve intent. If the compact crop loses the social relationship, use a separate select.

## 9. Delivery derivatives

- Archive: full-resolution TIFF or maximum-quality source.
- Master web grade: high-quality full-resolution JPEG/TIFF.
- Web: AVIF + WebP + JPEG fallback at required widths.
- Suggested widths: 480, 768, 1024, 1440, 1920 and 2560 only where justified.
- Social/OG: 1200 × 630 with safe text-free composition.
- Video: MP4/H.264 plus WebM where pipeline supports it, poster JPEG/AVIF, captions if speech exists.

The build pipeline generates derivatives; do not hand-author dozens of inconsistent exports.

## 10. Asset naming and handoff

```text
20g_[space]_[moment]_[subject]_[orientation]_[select]_[version]
```

Example: `20g_patio_evening_guests_wide_03_v2`.

Handoff includes contact sheet, selects, rights ledger, caption/credit sheet, crop/focal metadata and rejected/expired asset policy.

## 11. Visual reference request

Before final art direction, the client should provide:

- Existing high-resolution venue, food, bar, Patio, team and event imagery.
- Original brand guide/logo/font files.
- 5–10 references they admire, with notes about what they like—not only links.
- Any imagery they dislike or cannot legally reuse.

If these inputs are unavailable, schedule a half-day or full-day production rather than filling the site with generic stock. The final moodboard must use licensed/owned references and clearly label inspiration versus production target.

## 12. Production QA

- Every visual maps to a frame in the register.
- Menu items and staff shown are current at launch.
- No published subject lacks consent.
- Crops work at all target breakpoints.
- Text contrast is verified on actual crops.
- LCP image meets the weight budget.
- Alt text is contextual; decorative assets are marked accordingly.
- Reduced-motion poster frame is intentionally selected.
- No stock or generated image is presented as the venue.
